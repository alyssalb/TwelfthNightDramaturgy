Director's Statement

"Journeys end in lovers meeting."
Twelfth Night, Act 2, Scene 3

What if the place where you lose everything is also the place where you discover who you might become?

Welcome to Illyria: a sun-soaked island paradise full of luxury resorts, where old money, new money, and those just trying to get by collide beneath the palms. Inspired by the wonderfully unruly spirit of Key West, our Twelfth Night brings together an aristocratic heiress, a tech-bro real estate mogul, a pair of displaced Latine immigrants, and an assortment of lovable misfits in a glorious tangle of mistaken identities, misplaced affections, and unapologetic revelry.

At its heart, Twelfth Night is a play about the identities we inhabit, the identities we perform, and the possibilities that emerge when the world no longer requires us to be who we have always been. Our production will embrace the queerness woven into Shakespeare’s text, making it the baseline of our Illyria: a world where gender and sexuality need not be explained, labeled, or justified, where desire refuses to follow a predictable script, and where reinvention becomes an invitation to possibility.

For Viola, a gender-expansive Latine immigrant who survives a devastating shipwreck, Illyria offers refuge from the expectations of their former life. Assuming their brother’s clothing, they discover an opportunity to explore their masculinity, remain connected to their twin, and imagine a future beyond the boundaries of who they were expected to be. Their journey embodies the tension at the heart of this production: the longing to belong, the freedom to change, and the courage to embrace the parts of ourselves that the world may not know how to name.

Our inspiration extends to Key West’s Fantasy Fest, with its outrageous costumes, uninhibited self-expression, and joyful disruption of the ordinary. We embrace the camp, the carnivalesque, and the delicious absurdity of a world where everyone is performing a version of themselves. Beneath the farce and revelry, however, lies a genuine longing for connection. These characters are searching for love, belonging, and the freedom to be seen.

We are reimagining what it means for their ‘journeys (to) end in lovers meeting’. Rather than returning everyone to the comfort of a conventional, heteronormative resolution, we want to leave room for something more expansive. Orsino can fall for Viola without requiring them to abandon Cesario—to embrace the masculine and feminine qualities that coexist within them, without insisting they return to their “women’s weeds.” Olivia can recognize that with Sebastian comes Antonio, and that love need not be diminished by making room for one more person. Our ending will celebrate the possibility that happily-ever-after doesn't have to mean settling back into the identities and relationships society expects.

As a director, I believe the best work happens when artists feel empowered to take risks, follow their instincts, and make choices bold enough to fail fabulously. I will not always have all the answers, but I will always work with you to find them. We will begin with a week of intensive table work, exploring Shakespeare’s language, distinguishing prose from verse, and developing a shared understanding of the text, our characters, and their relationships. From there, we will build a world that balances the precision of farce with the emotional honesty beneath it, embracing theatrical play, collaborative discovery, and the freedom to surprise ourselves.

My hope is that we create a production that is as entertaining as it is affirming: one that invites audiences to laugh freely, recognize themselves in unexpected places, and imagine possibilities beyond the boundaries of convention.

Sometimes the storm that leaves us shipwrecked is the very thing that brings us home.

I’m so excited to embark on this journey with all of you!

“TK” (Tara) Kromer
Director, Twelfth Night
Theatre UCF 2026-27
